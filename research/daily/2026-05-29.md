# 2026-05-29 — The cellular automaton learns. All 8 levels differentiate.

## Proof of life

8 levels, stateless blocks, detached laterals, multi-rate firing ([1,1,2,4,8,16,32,64]), noise (σ=0.1), per-level prediction loss. CE: 3.54 → 2.30 (random baseline ~4.17, so 1.87 nats learned). All 8 prediction losses improve. Nothing collapses.

Config ([report_v1.json](../../experiments/automaton/artifacts/report_v1.json)):

```json
{"n_levels": 8, "steps_per_token": 4, "d_stream": 128,
 "seq_len": 2048, "batch_size": 8, "steps": 500,
 "noise_std": 0.1, "lr": 0.0003}
```

1,185,792 parameters. RTX 3090. ~3h17m total (~23.5s/step). Vectorized across levels via `bmm` — necessary because torch.compile/Triton unavailable on Windows (10x speedup over naive loop: 55s→5s/step at small scale).

## Per-level differentiation

The interesting result isn't the CE — it's that multi-rate structure creates measurable differentiation in prediction difficulty:

| Level | Rate | Step 10 MSE | Step 500 MSE | Events/batch | Reduction |
|-------|------|-------------|--------------|--------------|-----------|
| 0 | 1 | 0.0089 | 0.0046 | 8191 | 48% |
| 1 | 1 | 0.0072 | 0.0064 | 4095 | 11% |
| 2 | 2 | 0.0071 | 0.0040 | 2047 | 44% |
| 3 | 4 | 0.0071 | 0.0032 | 1023 | 54% |
| 4 | 8 | 0.0074 | 0.0034 | 511 | 54% |
| 5 | 16 | 0.0075 | 0.0037 | 255 | 51% |
| 6 | 32 | 0.0079 | 0.0040 | 127 | 49% |
| 7 | 64 | 0.0073 | 0.0043 | 63 | 40% |

From [run_v1.jsonl](../../experiments/automaton/artifacts/run_v1.jsonl). At step 10, all levels are ~uniform (0.007–0.009). By step 500, a **U-shaped pattern** emerges: L1 hardest (0.0064), L3–4 easiest (0.0032–0.0034), L6–7 rising again (0.0040–0.0043). Range is ~2× (0.003–0.006) — real but mild.

Tentative interpretation: L1 is hard because it receives L0's most volatile signal while firing half as often — phase misalignment. Mid-levels hit a sweet spot: smoother targets (longer timescale averages out token noise) with enough training events (~500–1000/batch). Upper levels are sample-starved (63–127 events/batch).

---

## Caveat: one-way flow

Code review of [automaton.py](../../core/automaton.py) confirms the model is **upward-only**. `arrivals[0]` is always zero — level 0 gets no lateral input from higher levels. CE measures level-0 performance alone; higher levels don't contribute to token prediction yet. The full bidirectional predictive coding story isn't implemented.

---

## What this doesn't yet show

- Whether noise *causes* the U-shaped differentiation (vs multi-rate timing alone)
- Whether levels learn genuinely different representations (vs converging to the same feature)
- Whether bidirectional flow improves CE

## What's next

Noise ablation: same config, `noise_std=0.0`. If the U-shape flattens, noise is doing something. If it persists, it's purely a multi-rate timing artifact.
