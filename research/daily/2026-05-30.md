# 2026-05-30 — Eager PyTorch bottoms out here; Triton is the path.

## The question that closed today

The saved eager profile says the model is paying for thousands of tiny launches, not one bad kernel. In [`profile_graph.ignore.log`](../../runs/profile_graph.ignore.log), `aten::bmm` is only about a third of self CUDA time; the rest is fragmented across elementwise work, adds, indexing, norms, and `where`.

```text

  aten::bmm                     83.644ms   33.83%
  vectorized_elementwise_kernel 64.411ms   26.05%
  aten::add_                    62.236ms   25.17%
```

That is enough to stop looking for a clever eager rewrite. The bottleneck is the per-timestep fragmentation itself. The discriminating next step is one fused Triton timestep kernel for neighbor read → normalize → MLP → prediction head → buffer write.

## InfoNCE beat the old local loss for the right reason

[`automaton_graph.py`](../../core/automaton_graph.py) now defaults to InfoNCE (commit `e712c9a`), and band 0 is explicitly exempt from the local prediction loss.

```python
loss_type: str = "infonce"
...
prediction_errors = self._prediction_errors(current_predictions, prediction_target.detach())
prediction_errors[self.band0_mask] = 0.0
```

The saved fixed-batch ablation in [`ablation_infonce.ignore.log`](../../runs/ablation_infonce.ignore.log) is small but clean:

| Step | MSE CE | InfoNCE CE |
|---|---:|---:|
| 19 | 2.5317 | **2.4093** |

InfoNCE is better by ~0.12 nats on the only metric that matters here: token CE. That makes the old MSE target look like unnecessary softness. The discriminating next step is to see whether that edge survives the 500-step run rather than only the 20-step overfit.

## Refractory made the dynamics less trivial and the CE better

The saved refractory ablation in [`ablation_refractory.ignore.log`](../../runs/ablation_refractory.ignore.log) goes the same way as the wave story: token prediction improves even though the local prediction task gets harder. The saved artifact does not record the refractory hyperparameters, so the claim here stays narrow.

| Step | Baseline CE | Refractory CE | Baseline pred | Refractory pred |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 19 | 2.5505 | **2.4565** | 1.1714 | 1.2911 |

That is what you would expect if refractory is turning uniform diffusion into a more structured transport problem. The discriminating next step is an impulse-response diagnostic: a moving shell would support the wave hypothesis; a broadening blob would kill it.

## The larger run finished — all bands learn, mid-rates learn best

The 1000-step run completed. Final metrics from [`graph_infonce_1000_clipped.ignore.log`](../../runs/graph_infonce_1000_clipped.ignore.log):

```text
step=1000  ce_loss=2.6866  total_prediction_loss=340.4644
  b1=1.6504  b2=1.7845  b3=1.8692  b4=1.3819  b5=1.3800  b6=2.6009  b7=3.5193
```

`log(64) ≈ 4.16`. Every band is well below that. Bands 4-5 (rates 16, 32) are the *best* performers at ~1.38 — better than bands 1-2 (~1.65). This inverts the initial ordering where lower bands learned first. The U-shaped pattern from the May 29 report is now an inverted U: **mid-rate bands learn the strongest local predictions, not the fastest-firing ones.**

Tentative interpretation: bands 4-5 fire rarely enough to have genuinely informative targets (the lateral signal has changed meaningfully between fires) but often enough to accumulate ~1000+ training events per 1000 steps. Bands 1-2 fire too often — their prediction targets barely change between fires, so the contrastive signal is weaker. Band 7 is sample-starved (only ~8 events per step).

A 5000-step run with `lr=1e-4` is now underway (`runs/graph_infonce_5000.ignore.log`). The discriminating next step is whether the CE curve has actually plateaued at ~2.65 or whether the lower LR pushes it further.
