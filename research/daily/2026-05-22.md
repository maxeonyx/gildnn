# 2026-05-22

Fixed multi-rate execution works. Not marginally — the result is clear: skip computation on a fixed schedule, reuse cached outputs, and get 15-21% real wall-clock speedup with *better* quality than running every block every step. The rate constraint acts as a regularizer, forcing blocks to produce longer-timescale representations.

## Multi-rate [1,1,2,4]: 14.8% speedup, quality better

Four blocks, rates [1, 1, 2, 4]. Rate-4 block executes every 4th timestep; on skipped steps, its cached output is MixAdd'd into the stream unchanged.

| Step | All-rate-1 val loss | Multi-rate val loss | Delta | Speedup |
|------|----:|----:|------:|------:|
| 500 | 2.188 | 2.171 | -0.017 | 11.2% |
| 1000 | 2.062 | 2.052 | -0.010 | 12.4% |

Final timing (1000 passes): **14.8%** speedup. Artifact: [`experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/short_run/report.json`](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/short_run/report.json).

## Multi-rate [1,2,4,8]: 20.7% speedup, quality better

More aggressive: each successive block halves its execution frequency. Rate-8 block computes 4 times across a 32-character context.

| Step | All-rate-1 val loss | Multi-rate val loss | Delta |
|------|----:|----:|------:|
| 500 | 2.188 | 2.168 | -0.020 |
| 1000 | 2.062 | 2.051 | -0.010 |
| 2000 | 1.946 | 1.941 | -0.006 |

Final timing (500 passes): all-rate-1 73.89ms, multi-rate 58.59ms → **20.7%** speedup. Artifact: [`experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/aggressive_1248/report.json`](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/aggressive_1248/report.json).

Quality advantage is consistent across all checkpoints (multi-rate always lower loss). Max's interpretation ([dictation 2026-05-22-10](../../dictations/2026-05-22-10.md)): the rate constraint pressures blocks toward "predicting further in the future" — their output must be useful across multiple timesteps.

## Why this works where selective execution failed

Selective execution (learned gating): 26-29% *slower* — the gating overhead exceeds the savings from skipping cheap blocks. Fixed multi-rate: zero decision overhead (the schedule is a compile-time constant). The full compute reduction translates directly to wall-clock savings.

## Diagonal + multi-rate: suggestive but not settled

Adding diagonal connections (block A's output delta at time t feeds block B at time t+1) to multi-rate [1,2,4,8]:

| Step | Control (multi-rate) | Diagonal | Delta |
|------|--:|--:|--:|
| 500 | 2.192 | 2.169 | -0.022 |
| 1000 | 2.066 | 2.064 | -0.002 |
| 1500 | 1.983 | 1.992 | +0.009 |
| 2000 | 1.988 | 1.935 | **-0.052** |

The -0.052 at step 2000 is large, but the trajectory is non-monotonic (crosses zero at step 1500). The control's val loss worsened in the last 500 steps while diagonal kept improving — consistent with a stabilization/regularization effect, but could also be seed variance.

Not settled. Needs longer run or multi-seed to confirm. Artifact: [`experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/diagonal_1248_vs_baseline/report.json`](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/diagonal_1248_vs_baseline/report.json).
