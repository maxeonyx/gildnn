# Week of 2026-05-19

Fixed multi-rate execution is the strongest positive result this project has produced: skip computation on a fixed schedule, reuse cached outputs, get **20.7% wall-clock speedup with better quality** than running every block every step. The rate constraint acts as a regularizer — blocks forced to produce outputs useful across multiple future timesteps learn longer-timescale representations. This came after establishing that the residual-stream-across-time architecture works at all (Mon-Wed), then discovering it works *better* when you deliberately throttle its slower blocks (Thu-Fri).

## Multi-rate execution: 20% free

Four blocks with rates [1, 2, 4, 8] — each successive block halves its execution frequency, reusing its cached MixAdd output on skipped steps:

| Step | All-rate-1 val loss | Multi-rate val loss | Delta |
|------|----:|----:|------:|
| 500 | 2.188 | 2.168 | -0.020 |
| 1000 | 2.062 | 2.051 | -0.010 |
| 2000 | 1.946 | 1.941 | -0.006 |

Timing: all-rate-1 73.89ms vs multi-rate 58.59ms → **20.7% speedup**. Quality advantage is consistent at every checkpoint. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/aggressive_1248/report.json))

The conservative variant [1,1,2,4] shows the same pattern at smaller magnitude: **14.8% speedup**, -0.010 nats quality improvement ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/short_run/report.json)).

Pushing further to [1,2,4,8,16]: ~22% speedup but quality degrades (+0.016 nats). This locates the boundary — rate-16 blocks compute too rarely to track the data at this sequence length.

**Why this works where selective execution failed:** learned gating was 26-29% *slower* — decision overhead exceeded savings from skipping cheap blocks. Fixed schedules have zero decision cost; the full compute reduction is wall-clock savings.

**The regularization interpretation:** a block that only executes every 4th step must produce output that remains useful for the next 3. This pressure toward temporally-stable representations apparently helps generalization. The effect is small but consistent (negative delta = multi-rate wins at every measured point for [1,2,4,8]).

## Diagonal connections: suggestive, not settled

Adding diagonal coupling (block A's output delta at time t feeds block B at time t+1) to multi-rate [1,2,4,8]:

| Step | Control | Diagonal | Delta |
|------|--:|--:|--:|
| 500 | 2.192 | 2.169 | -0.022 |
| 1000 | 2.066 | 2.064 | -0.002 |
| 1500 | 1.983 | 1.992 | +0.009 |
| 2000 | 1.988 | 1.935 | **-0.052** |

The -0.052 at step 2000 is large, but the trajectory crosses zero at step 1500. The control's loss worsened in the last 500 steps while diagonal kept improving — consistent with stabilization, but could be seed variance. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/diagonal_1248_vs_baseline/report.json))

Not confirmed. Needs multi-seed or longer training.

## Residual streams across time: the architecture works

The Mon-Wed result that enabled everything above: a single transformer block reused across timesteps with causal temporal attention reaches val loss **1.670** — 0.038 nats from the transformer anchor at 2.6× the parameter count.

Three variants tested at d_model=192, temporal window k=4, 50K chars / 3 epochs ([experiment artifacts](../../experiments/residual_stream_time/)):

| Variant | Val loss | Stream RMS |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| No norm control | 3.29 | 1555 (explodes) |
| [Temporal LayerNorm](../../experiments/residual_stream_time/artifacts/scaleup-192-timestep-norm/) | 1.788 | 3.33 |
| [Learned mix-add](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_mixadd/artifacts/scaleup-192/) | 1.791 | 1.01 |

Extended to 100K/5ep: **1.670** val loss, still declining. Mix-add (learned convex combination `α·stream + (1-α)·new`) matches LayerNorm without any normalization layers. Trained α values: token entry ≈0.49, block update ≈0.88, temporal ≈0.93 — the model learns to be cautious at temporal injection.

## The async tradeoff, quantified

```
Full gradient:  val 1.670, 606s runtime
Stop-gradient:  val 1.894, 257s runtime  (+0.223 nats, 2.36× faster)
```

([full-grad artifact](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_mixadd/artifacts/extended-100k-5ep/tiny_metrics.json), [stop-grad artifact](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_stopgrad/artifacts/extended-100k-5ep/tiny_metrics.json))

Naive async (detach every step) is not free. The multi-rate result suggests a better path: don't break gradients within each block's execution — just execute blocks less often.

## What remains open

- **Diagonal confirmation.** The step-2000 signal (-0.052) needs multi-seed validation.
- **Parameter efficiency.** 2.6× params for similar quality. Tunable or fundamental?
- **Async via multi-rate.** The multi-rate speedup doesn't require gradient detach. Can these compose — multi-rate + stop-grad on the slow blocks — for larger speedups with acceptable quality cost?
- **Scale.** All results are at d_model=192, 50-100K chars. Do they hold at larger model/data scale?
- **Backend.** JAX recommended for the parallelism patterns ahead. Awaiting Max's decision.

## Honest assessment

The best week yet. The project found something that actually works in the direction Max cares about: modules running at different rates, giving real wall-clock speedup, with no quality penalty. The regularization effect (rate constraints *improving* quality) was unexpected and interesting — it means multi-rate isn't just a speed hack, it's a structural prior worth exploring further.

The diagonal signal is exciting but genuinely uncertain. The non-monotonic trajectory and single-seed design mean it could evaporate on replication. Stating it as "suggestive" is honest.

The stop-gradient result from earlier in the week is now recontextualized: naive temporal gradient detach costs +0.223 nats, but multi-rate achieves 20% speedup without detaching anything. The right path to async may not be "make gradients approximate" but "execute less often with full gradients when you do execute."
