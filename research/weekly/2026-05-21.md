# Week of 2026-05-19

Residual streams across time work. A single transformer block, reused across timesteps, attending causally over its own past states, produces recognizable English at val loss 1.79. The architecture Max actually wants to explore is viable. That's this week's headline.

## The architecture probe

Three variants of the same idea — one residual block at d_model=192, temporal attention window k=4, trained 3 epochs on 50K chars ([experiment artifacts](../../experiments/residual_stream_time/)):

| Variant | Val loss | Stream RMS | Outcome |
|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| No norm control | 3.29 | 1555 | Broken — stream explodes |
| [Temporal LayerNorm](../../experiments/residual_stream_time/artifacts/scaleup-192-timestep-norm/) | 1.788 | 3.33 | Works |
| [Learned mix-add](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_mixadd/artifacts/scaleup-192/) | 1.791 | 1.01 | Matches LN, no normalization layers |

Transformer anchor: 1.63 at 186K params. These models are 480K params and undertrained (loss still declining at termination). The gap might be training budget; might be architectural. Not yet discriminated.

**Norm management is binary.** Without it, the residual stream magnitude grows without bound across timesteps. Attention saturates. The model outputs near-uniform. With either mechanism keeping the stream bounded, the same architecture drops 1.5 nats. This isn't a tuning knob — it's a gate between "works" and "doesn't work at all."

**Mix-add solves norm without normalization layers.** Learned scalar convex combination at each injection: `stream = α·stream + (1-α)·new_value`. After training ([model_summary.json](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_mixadd/artifacts/scaleup-192/model_summary.json)): token entry α≈0.49, block update α≈0.88, temporal update α≈0.93. The model learns heavy weighting on the existing stream at the temporal injection — cautious about overwriting.

**Diagonal coupling is inconclusive.** Block i at time T feeding block i+1 at time T+1 (Max's "main thing I want to explore"): [2-block diagonal](../../experiments/residual_stream_time_diagonal/artifacts/scaleup-192/) gives val loss 1.788 at 777K params — identical to single-block at 481K. Doesn't hurt, doesn't help at this scale. Remains a live hypothesis for larger models.

## The GRU async calibration (before reorientation)

Six experiments on Monday calibrated async stale-read execution on GRU modules. The headline number from [5-seed 900k/100k calibration](../../experiments/async_gru_corpus/artifacts/multiseed_corpus_report.json):

```
Mean async quality penalty: +0.0055 ± 0.0046 nats (95% CI crosses zero)
```

Mechanically stable. Tiny cost. But GRUs aren't the architecture Max wants — these results establish that stale-read semantics work in general, not that any specific architecture is ready.

Other GRU-async findings this week:
- Broadcast channel (mean-pool global read): [actively hurt](../../experiments/broadcast_channel/) (+0.005 worse than plain async)
- Scale-up to 11M params on 100k data: gap disappears into data-saturation noise (mean -0.003 ± 0.009 across 5 seeds)

## What remains open

- **Parameter efficiency.** The probe uses 2.6× more params than the transformer anchor for worse loss. Training budget or architecture? Longer runs will discriminate.
- **Diagonal coupling.** Needs more blocks or larger d_model to have room to matter.
- **Async speed demonstration.** Stale reads are stable but the actual FLOPs/sec advantage Max expects hasn't been demonstrated yet. That requires architectural parallelism (multiple blocks executing independently), not just tolerating staleness.
- **Training speed.** The residual-stream-time model has a sequential timestep dependency that makes it ~10× slower than transformer training. Whether this is inherent or amortizable is open.

## Process corrections

The [reorientation dictation](../../dictations/2026-05-20-15.md) changed the project's direction mid-week. Key shifts:

1. **Theory first.** New experiments now require a written design document reviewed by thinker before code runs. The [residual-stream-across-time report](../questions/residual-stream-across-time/README.md) is the first artifact of this process.
2. **Stop using GRUs as the default primitive.** Max's interest is residual streams, attention over time, and learned mix-add — not stock gating mechanisms.
3. **Async means speed, not accuracy.** The expectation is FLOPs/sec improvement from true parallel execution, not quality benefits.
4. **Experiment details matter.** Reports should show what was tried, what subtle changes mattered, not just final numbers.

The theory-first process produced a cleaner experiment than anything before it.

These are all "needs more scale" problems, which is exactly what next week should test.
