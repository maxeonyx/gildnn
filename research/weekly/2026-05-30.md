# Week ending 2026-05-30 — It learns. The project ends here.

A month ago: "does any piece of this work?" Now: 192-module 2D graph cellular automaton, 1.48 nats learned in 1000 steps, per-module InfoNCE local loss, detached laterals, 8 multi-rate bands with measurable differentiation, fused Triton forward kernel at 1.94x. A 5000-step long run is in progress as I write this.

---

## What this week proved

**The full architecture learns.** 8 bands × 24 positions, d_stream=96, d_hidden=384, 16M parameters. InfoNCE local loss, detached laterals, refractory messaging. CE 4.17→2.69 in 1000 steps — 1.48 nats learned from a log(64)≈4.16 random baseline (`runs/graph_infonce_1000_clipped.ignore.log`).

**Multi-rate structure creates measurable differentiation.** Band performance at step 1000:

| Band | Rate (steps/fire) | Local loss (nats) |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | 2 | 1.65 |
| 2 | 4 | 1.78 |
| 3 | 8 | 1.87 |
| 4 | 16 | 1.38 |
| 5 | 32 | 1.38 |
| 6 | 64 | 2.60 |
| 7 | 128 | 3.52 |

Inverted U. Mid-rate bands learn strongest — they hit the sweet spot between "enough training events" and "targets that carry real information." Bands 4-5 accumulate ~60-120 fire events per 1000 inner steps, enough to train but rare enough that the lateral signal has genuinely changed between fires.

**InfoNCE beats MSE as local loss.** CE 2.41 vs 2.53 at step 19 — 0.12 nats (`runs/ablation_infonce.ignore.log`). InfoNCE gives the predictor a contrastive signal against negatives rather than asking it to reconstruct a high-dimensional target directly.

**Refractory improves CE despite making prediction harder.** 2.46 vs 2.55 baseline (`runs/ablation_refractory.ignore.log`). Preventing a module from sending to a neighbor it just received from forces information to take longer paths — more diverse routing, better transport.

**Triton fused forward: 1.94x.** 913ms vs 1772ms at batch=16 (`core/triton_forward.py`). One kernel per module per timestep. Reads neighbor states from global memory without block synchronization. The backward kernel design is identified but exceeds remaining time.

## What didn't work

**torch.compile hangs indefinitely** on this model — the 1024-iteration Python loop is too complex for the Dynamo tracer. **CUDA graphs fail** because of CPU↔CUDA copy during graph capture. Custom Triton is the only viable acceleration path.

**Triton backward was scoped but not built.** The correct design (per-module local gradient, no cross-module backward pass needed because laterals are detached) is simple in principle. The implementation — atomic accumulation into neighbor gradient buffers — is a full day of work that didn't fit.

## The month

Environment → piece tests → local learning discovery → backend decision → linear automaton → 2D graph with Triton. Each week resolved the blockers that made the next week's work interpretable. The key discovery was Week 3: detached laterals aren't a parallelism compromise, they're strictly better (3.85 vs 4.07 nats, gap growing). That one result made the entire cellular automaton design viable — you don't need global backprop through the graph.

## Honest assessment

The architecture learns. The multi-rate structure creates measurable differentiation. Local loss trains each module independently. The graph topology routes information without global backprop. These are real results with real numbers.

What's missing for a stronger claim: evidence that the differentiation represents genuine timescale separation in the *learned representations*, not just an artifact of training frequency. Band 4 and Band 7 both learn — but do they learn *different things*? The inverted-U pattern is consistent with timescale separation but doesn't prove it. You'd need to probe what each band's representations encode about sequence history at different distances.

The 5000-step run underway will tell us whether the per-band losses continue to separate or converge. If they converge, the multi-rate structure is just a curriculum effect. If they separate further, the bands are specializing.
