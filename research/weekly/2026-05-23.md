# Week of 2026-05-22

Width is the parallel architecture's natural scaling axis. A 4-block d=256 parallel model reaches 1.718 val_loss in 204 seconds — beating a 6-block d=256 sequential model (1.737, 593s) in both quality and wall-time. Multi-rate parallel wins at matched FLOPs (+0.019 avg, 3-seed). The compute frontier is mapped. But we've hit TinyShakespeare's ceiling, we still have no transformer baseline, and the real research questions — local learning, many-block scaling — remain conceptual rather than operational.

## Parallel architecture: validated

Last week's matched-FLOP result was initially a loss for parallel. This week's replication with the correct architecture — true parallel blocks with lateral (diagonal) propagation, Max's actual vision — reverses that:

```
All-block readout:  1.793 val_loss (20K steps)
Sequential control: 1.784
Gap:                +0.010 (noise-level, within seed variance)
```

([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/parallel_diagonal_variants_20k/))

The all-block readout closes 89% of the gap vs sequential. Not a win on its own — but this is the architecture that *can* scale, because blocks execute independently.

The matched-FLOP test settles it: bigger blocks that fire less often (+0.019 avg improvement over sequential at equal compute, 3-seed). ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/parallel_diagonal_matched_flop_fixed_3seed/))

## Width scaling: the major result

| Config | Val loss | Wall time | FLOPs |
|--------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Parallel 4-block d=256 | **1.718** | **204s** | — |
| Sequential 6-block d=256 | 1.737 | 593s | — |
| Sequential 2-block d=256 | ~1.76 | — | — |

([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/width_scaling/))

Parallel dominates on both axes. The mechanism: parallel blocks share a stream and execute concurrently; wider d_model gives each block more representational capacity without increasing depth or sequential dependency. Sequential pays a 3× wall-time cost for worse quality.

This is the scaling story: more capacity per block, not more blocks in sequence.

## Compute frontier

Parallel 4-block at 123K FLOPs beats sequential 2-block at 131K FLOPs. But sequential dominates at high compute. Parallel plateaus past 4 blocks at d=128. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/compute_frontier_sweep/))

The crossover means parallel's advantage is structural (independence, speed) not raw capacity. At high compute where sequential can afford depth, depth wins — at this scale, on this dataset.

## Rate dilation: context-dependent

At ctx=32, aggressive rates [1,2,4,16] ≈ [1,2,4,8]. A wash — rate=32 fires exactly once in a 32-token context. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/rate_dilation_sweep/))

At ctx=128, wide spacing [1,4,16,32] beats narrow [1,2,4,8] (1.803 vs 1.812). Wide temporal hierarchy IS useful when slow blocks get 4+ firings. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/context_scaling/))

## Context scaling: the bottleneck exposed

ctx=128 is **worse** than ctx=32 overall (best ctx=128: 1.796 vs ctx=32: 1.718). Root cause: compute per token.

The multi-rate architecture saves compute — which works against it when the task needs MORE compute per token. Sequential 6-block at ctx=128 reaches 1.716 (better than any ctx=32 result), proving the task benefits from longer context given enough capacity.

Temporal window scaling confirmed this isn't an attention reach problem: window=16 gives 0.042 improvement over window=4, but full-sequence attention (window=128) gives only marginal further gain. The model sees the context — it just can't process it deeply enough. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/temporal_window_scaling/))

## Self-prediction: negative

Zero task effect at every lambda tested. Blocks specialize naturally without auxiliary prediction losses pushing them to. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/self_prediction_sweep/))

One less knob to tune. Also suggests the multi-rate constraint alone is sufficient for block specialization — no need for explicit hierarchical objectives at this scale.

## Cosine LR / longer training: negative

Cosine schedule does not prevent overfitting. The model memorizes TinyShakespeare before LR decays meaningfully. Overfitting peaks at 35-40K steps regardless of schedule. ([artifact](../../experiments/fixed_multi_rate/artifacts/cosine_lr_longer/))

TinyShakespeare is the ceiling, not the schedule.

## d=384 dead spot: diagnosed

Not structural. Pure overfitting: peaks at 1.732 (step 14K) then train-val gap explodes. µP predicts optimal LR ∝ 1/width → d=384 wants LR ~2e-4. Fixable but not worth fixing on TinyShakespeare.

## What's missing

**No transformer baseline.** Every comparison this project has made is internal — parallel vs sequential, multi-rate vs all-rate-1. We don't know how any of this compares to a standard transformer of equivalent size. This is the most important gap.

**Limited block count.** Max's vision is many parallel blocks (dozens, hundreds), not 4. We've only tested up to 4. The plateau at 4 blocks might be a d=128 artifact or might be fundamental.

**TinyShakespeare ceiling.** Every experiment saturates at 20-40K steps. Real scaling behavior — whether width continues to dominate, whether many-block parallelism pays off — is invisible at this data scale.

**Local learning unoperationalized.** Per [dictation 2026-05-23-5](../../dictations/2026-05-23-5.md): the actual research question is how to learn without global backprop, enabling true parallelism at scale. This remains a concept, not a concrete experiment design with alternatives to test.

**Hierarchical prediction untested.** Higher blocks predicting latent features rather than raw tokens. No experiment design exists.

## Honest assessment

The architecture works and the scaling story is legible: width is the knob, parallel dominates wall-time, quality is competitive or better at matched compute. These are real results with multi-seed replication.

But the intellectual work hasn't kept pace with the experimental throughput. We've been grinding through parameter sweeps on a toy dataset without advancing the hard questions. Local learning, hierarchical prediction, many-block scaling — these need *thinking*, not more TinyShakespeare runs. The matched-FLOP win is satisfying but ultimately just confirms "parallel is a valid architecture" — which is table stakes for the actual research.

Next week needs to shift mode: design concrete experiments for local learning (what are the alternatives? what would success look like? what's the minimal test?), stand up a transformer baseline (should have existed from day one), and move to a dataset that doesn't saturate in 20 minutes of training.
