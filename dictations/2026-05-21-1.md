## Architecture: mix-add over layer norm for residual stream management

The agent is using layer norm to fix residual stream explosion. I find this personally quite disgusting. To me, residual stream explosion should be managed by not increasing the norm accidentally when adding residuals. That's the whole point of my mix-add operator. And there's three variants of the mix-add:

1. **Plain mix-add** with a fixed parameter — a hyperparameter.
2. **Learned mix-add** with a learnable but static parameter.
3. **Dynamic mix-add** with a data-derived learned parameter.

Static mix-add is obviously correct to do compared to just adding residuals. I think learned mix-add is the best, though. That lets the RNN set its own forgetting threshold, essentially, in the residual stream. That seems pretty clear to me. But data-dependent mix-add is pretty cool. I don't know what that means as a gating mechanism — I personally don't think that these things need to be theoretically based. I see the other gating mechanisms and I'm thinking they're insanely complicated. Just add, but just add has your residual stream explosion problem because you're changing the norm when adding — when you add two normally distributed sets you expand the variance of that set. So it's not quite what you want. You do need to scale by the dimensionality of the two sides and the dimensionality of the output.

I would like to see attempts to do model architectures without layer norm, batch norm, these kind of things. I kind of think that they're hacks unless they're justified.
