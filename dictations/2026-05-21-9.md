# Dictation 2026-05-21-9

This is a side direction, not the main focus — but it's something that would be part of building my own ideal neural network training process. Perfect to me, maybe "special" to others.

The idea: start off with extremely high, artificially high batch sizes using gradient accumulation, then slowly tune the batch size down and the learning rate down equivalently. You go through noisier gradients later on, but with smaller changes in the position of the weights.

The intuition is that early in training you're far from a good solution so large steps and stable gradient estimates are fine. Later you're near a minimum, and smaller steps with noisier gradients act as a kind of regularization — stopping you from overfitting too hard to the training signal. Declining LR and declining batch size together, kept in some kind of equivalence with each other.

Interesting connection: Adam and AdamW already have their own internal per-parameter rate adaptation — they divide each update by a running estimate of the gradient magnitude, so parameters with large gradients get smaller effective steps automatically. That's a form of implicit scheduling already happening inside the optimizer. Muon doesn't do this — it's closer to SGD with momentum in that sense, which is part of why switching to Muon requires recalibrating the learning rate. So the relationship between explicit LR schedules and what the optimizer is already doing internally is worth thinking about.
