# Experiment directions — local learning, GPU utilization, async, attention residuals

Local learning and stop gradients can be experimented with without the asynchrony. That's essentially the improved version of the predictive chain example, where instead of going from A to B, we simply go from A to A and there's just bits hanging off the side that and we hope it actually do something. We have to experiment with a variety of different configurations of the local module. What is a local module? What is the boundary? That it's not quite clear. Like probably it's three nodes in the chain. I say chain, but I don't like the word chain because it doesn't go from one end to the other. The question is in that case, do we get a model that is better than a single module model? And does adding additional modules improve performance? And also, what performance can we get compared to other networks of the same parameters and or of the same training time in terms of flops, but also in terms of wall clock time? Though I do tend to think that we can get significantly more flops out of a GPU training an RNN than we can a transformer, I might be wrong, I don't know. In fact, there's a whole research avenue of this which is just understanding what kind of GPU programs fit best in the RTX 3090. I can't believe we haven't written that down yet. That's obvious.

The async/desynchronized stuff is kind of hard, but I'm interested to see how it would work. Like even just a tiny example. It's not a network that is even trained, but or like just yeah, something something that we could train with stop gradients or overlapping stop gradients.

Separately, the more important async question: how can we run modules in parallel and have volatile memory in between them. So they don't have to synchronise with each other at the GPU level. That's the more important part. We want to be able to train without synchrony.

I'd like to see an attention residual transformer, please. I'd like you to train one of those. Multiple variants, one where it's attention over the depth, and another one where every attention model is attentive over depth and sequence length in a causal triangle. So it can't attend to the same layer in previous step. Why do we want to show that? Well, because we want to parallelize across time by adding only one depth at each time step for our RNN. For our transformer, it's not so important. We can do much more parallelization across the sequence length. But I don't personally want to see transformers too much.
