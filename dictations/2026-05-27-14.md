# Dictation 2026-05-27-14

## Future direction: learned distribution family

The stream in the predictive processing architecture carries distributions. Open question: what distribution family to use. One direction worth exploring later:

A learned distribution family with 4 components:

1. **Density evaluator** — a small frozen model: `(θ, x) → logp(x|θ)`. Like a transformer with parameters and an explicit query point.
2. **Sampler** — a small frozen model: `(θ, noise) → x ~ p_θ`.
3. **Combiner** — learned, trained with the architecture: `(θ_a, θ_b) → θ_post`. Stream accumulation / Bayesian-like update.
4. **Distance** — learned, IS the loss function: `(θ_a, θ_b) → scalar`. Backprop through it for gradients on θ. Could also learn a direct gradient predictor on top, but autograd through learned distance is the grounded version.

The key insight: rather than committing to a standard family (Gaussian, exponential), you train a flexible density model, freeze it, and then learn the combining/comparison operations in its parameter space. The family doesn't need exact Bayesian closure — just approximately good combining that you train explicitly (including multi-step rollouts, order-swapped examples, etc.).

This is a future direction, not current work. The immediate architecture can start with simpler distribution representations (diagonal Gaussians, explicit params) and upgrade later.
