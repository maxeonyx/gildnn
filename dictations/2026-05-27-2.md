## What "sanity check a piece" means

A sanity check is: does this piece learn at all? Is the loss going down? Is the architecture not completely broken? That takes 30-60 seconds. It does NOT mean training a complete model to convergence and analyzing the result as if it were meaningful.

## What "my models" means

The architecture I care about has higher-level blocks predicting stuff further in the future. That's why the design should work at long sequences — the higher blocks NEED long sequences because their prediction targets are far ahead. A flat GRU with TBPTT is not my model. A standard transformer is not my model. Testing those tells me nothing about whether my design works.

My architecture: multi-block grid, blocks as temporal edges, stale laterals (one-timestep delay), noise creating information hierarchy, higher blocks predict further ahead with local predictive loss, distributional stream with weight-tied readout as the combining mechanism. Those are the pieces. Sanity check them individually, then assemble.

## The assembly

We should be assembling now. The individual pieces have been poked at for days. Put them together. If a piece turns out broken during assembly, fix it then. But stop endlessly testing pieces in isolation against standard baselines — that's not getting us anywhere.
