# Dictation 2026-05-27-10

## Terminology: no "states"

Veto the word "states." States implies the internal states of an RNN. There is no such thing in our architecture. Well, there might be later, but there's not. There's only residual streams at certain times, at certain lateral distances.

## Get onto predictive processing NOW

Please get onto the predictive processing as soon as possible. I don't want to see more of these half-baked models with global backprop, "local" CE loss, which is just all of the blocks individually predicting token embeddings or token logits. I don't want all of the blocks predicting token logits. If I want that, I can use a transformer and I'm done.

No. I want to see a biologically inspired neural network. I want to see something that has hierarchical processing at different time scales. I want to see all of the pieces, though, proven somehow in a clever way, independently. Not by training yet another model that isn't what I like. Not a full fucking model. Don't train a full fucking model. Don't even train. Just do the test with some distribution of activations. Pass it through the block or the operation that you're testing and see that it behaves with the properties that you want.

## What predictive processing looks like

Block 0 predicts a distribution. Not logits. Not softmax. A distribution. Block 1 tries to predict that distribution. There's one time step of delay, so it tries to predict block 0's distribution from the time step that is one ahead of where block 1's inputs are when it started producing the prediction. The two get compared. The natural way to compare two distributions is Wasserstein distance. Depending on the distribution class, that's intractable. So we approximate it or we limit the distribution class. I don't care. Try a bunch of options.

Then, what propagates up and down as a result of combining the two? First of all, that was the loss. Second: what propagates in the actual operation that combines the two? What propagates up is surprisal — what wasn't predicted. What propagates down is something else. I don't know 100%. It's kind of the other information. What propagates up is what wasn't predicted. What propagates down — maybe that's what was predicted.

I don't know what that operation is, but I want to see a network that includes it. I want to see that operation tested too. I want to see you sample a whole bunch of shit from a class of distributions and put it through this operation. I want to see the loss tested in isolation, not through training, through validating that it does what you expect. That could be training, but I suggest at this point do not do that.

## All pieces are load-bearing simultaneously

The architecture I'm looking for doesn't make sense unless it has local learning. Local learning doesn't make sense unless it has the predictive structure. The predictive structure doesn't make sense unless there's multiple time steps per token and/or very long sequences.

It's not a dependency chain. If you omit any of those pieces, what you get is a nonsensical model. The pieces need to be tested in isolation, but if you take out any of the pieces, the model is not anything — it's useless.
